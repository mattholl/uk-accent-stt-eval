I'm going to start at the cradle with Emma O'Connell's poem taken from the anthology of West Riding dialect verse, A Babby in t'house. If your tea isn't ready or you're feeling done up, it's no use to grumble and grouse. There's nobody no time to take notice of you, not now there's a babby in t' house. You can't get to t'fire, the zippin's to dry. You have to crawl down as quiet as a mouse. You can't listen in t' football results. Hey, no, there's a babby in t'house. You daren't even whistle to t'dog when it's out. Even cat's forgotten to crass. And if somebody's t'cheek to knock at your door, it's shh, there's a babby in t' house. If you cower in a corner and light up your pipe, it's no comfort, it's nobbut a farce. For t'least smell o' rick gets you nowt but black looks because there's a babby in t' house. I know that we promised 'em when we were wed we'd tak' 'em for better or worse. It seems all one sort, I'm beginning to think, since we'd a babby in t' house. But I think it's like me, some colour in, and like me its hair's a bit sparse. Takin' all things with all it'd be a bit stale if we hadn't a babby in't house.
